# NIH application anatomy

Stable structure of NIH applications and review. Page limits and attachments below are the usual defaults; the NOFO and the current application guide control. Volatile rules are in `nih-policy-watch.md`.

## 1. Choosing a mechanism

| Code | Use it for | Typical shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | A mature, hypothesis-driven project with preliminary data | Up to 5 years; modular budget up to $250k direct per year, detailed above that; 12-page Research Strategy | The standard. Needs evidence the team can do the work. |
| R21 | Exploratory, higher-risk, early-stage ideas | 2 years, $275k direct total; 6 pages | Preliminary data not required, but reviewers reward it. Not every institute accepts the parent R21. |
| R03 | Small, self-contained project, pilot or secondary analysis | 2 years, $50k direct per year; 6 pages | Institute participation varies. |
| R15 (AREA/REAP) | Research at institutions with limited NIH funding | 3 years, $300k direct total | Eligibility is institutional and by school; check whether the applicant's UNM unit qualifies before suggesting it. |
| R34 | Clinical trial planning | 1 to 3 years | Institute-specific. |
| R61/R33, UG3/UH3 | Phased projects with milestones | Milestone-gated transition | Milestones must be quantitative and credible. |
| K01, K08, K23, K99/R00, K24 | Mentored or mid-career development | 3 to 5 years, salary plus research support | The candidate, mentors and career plan carry as much weight as the science. |
| F30, F31, F32 | Individual predoctoral and postdoctoral fellowships | Stipend-based | Fellowship application and review were restructured in 2025; use the current guide. |
| T32 and other T | Institutional training programs | Tables-heavy | Not counted toward the six-application limit. |
| P01, P30, P50, U19, U54, U01 | Program projects, centers, cooperative agreements | Multi-component | Each component has its own page limits; overall and core sections matter. |
| R13 | Conferences | | Not counted toward the six-application limit. |
| Supplements | Diversity, administrative, re-entry | Tied to a parent award | Availability changed in 2025 and 2026; verify the notice is active. |

Decision questions: How much preliminary data exists? Is the PI early-stage? Is this a clinical trial under NIH's definition (it determines which NOFOs are allowed)? Which institute's mission does it fit, and has the PI talked to a program officer? Is there a targeted NOFO with set-aside funds, which often has better odds than a parent announcement?

## 2. IDeA-state options for New Mexico

New Mexico is an IDeA state, so UNM investigators can be part of NIGMS Institutional Development Award programs that most institutions cannot:
- **COBRE**: thematic centers that support junior investigators as project leads, with cores and mentoring. A junior UNM investigator may be able to get a COBRE project or pilot before an R01.
- **INBRE**: statewide research network including primarily undergraduate institutions.
- **IDeA-CTR / clinical and translational networks**, and IDeA co-funding of meritorious R01 or R15 applications that miss an institute's payline.

Check `unm-data/units/research-centers-and-cores.md` for which of these UNM currently holds. When writing environment or career sections for a junior investigator, existing IDeA infrastructure is concrete evidence of institutional support.

## 3. What goes in an application

Core research application (R series). Defaults:

| Component | Limit | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Project Summary/Abstract | 30 lines | Public summary; states the problem, objective, aims, approach, payoff |
| Project Narrative | 2 to 3 sentences | Public health relevance in lay terms |
| Introduction (resubmission or revision only) | 1 page | Response to the prior review |
| Specific Aims | 1 page | The whole case in miniature |
| Research Strategy | 12 pages (R01), 6 (R21, R03) | Significance, Innovation, Approach |
| Bibliography | no limit | |
| Facilities & Other Resources | no limit | How the environment makes the work feasible |
| Equipment | no limit | Major items available |
| Biosketches | 5 pages each | Use the current Common Form via SciENcv |
| Budget and justification | | Modular or detailed (R&R) |
| Human Subjects and Clinical Trials form | | Protection, inclusion, recruitment, timeline, and for trials the protocol synopsis and DSMP |
| Vertebrate Animals | | Description, justification, minimization of pain, euthanasia |
| Multiple PD/PI Leadership Plan | | Required for MPI |
| Letters of Support | | Collaborators, consultants, institutional commitments |
| Resource Sharing, Data Management and Sharing Plan | DMS Plan about 2 pages | Required for research generating scientific data |
| Authentication of Key Biological and/or Chemical Resources | 1 page suggested | |
| Consortium/Contractual | | Subaward documents |

K applications add: Candidate Information and Goals for Career Development (shares a 12-page limit with the Research Strategy), plans for mentoring, training in responsible conduct of research, statements from mentors and co-mentors (6 pages), letters of reference, Description of Institutional Environment (1 page) and Institutional Commitment to Candidate's Research Career Development (1 page). The last two are where UNM data matters most: protected time, start-up, space, and the departmental commitment must be specific and signed off by the chair.

Formatting defaults: 11-point or larger Arial, Georgia, Helvetica or Palatino Linotype; half-inch margins; no more than 15 characters per inch and 6 lines per inch; no URLs in page-limited sections unless the NOFO allows; PDF attachments.

## 4. How applications are reviewed and scored

Simplified framework for most research project grants (due dates from January 25, 2025):

- **Factor 1, Importance of the Research** (Significance and Innovation), scored 1 to 9.
- **Factor 2, Rigor and Feasibility** (Approach, and inclusion and clinical trial timeline where applicable), scored 1 to 9.
- **Factor 3, Expertise and Resources** (Investigators and Environment), rated sufficient or insufficient, with explanation only if insufficient.

The overall impact score (1 to 9, then averaged and multiplied by 10 to give 10 to 90) reflects the likelihood of sustained, powerful influence on the field. It is a judgment, not an average of the factors.

What this means for writing:
- Factors 1 and 2 are where points are won. The framework was designed to reduce reputational bias, so a famous team and a strong institution no longer lift a weak idea, and an unknown team is no longer penalized if expertise and resources are adequate.
- Factor 3 is pass/fail, but "insufficient" is damaging. The job of the biosketches, Facilities and letters is to remove any doubt, concisely. For UNM applicants this means showing that the specific expertise, cores, patient access and collaborators exist, not listing everything the university owns.
- Career development, fellowship, training and center applications use different criteria. Read the review section of the NOFO.

Additional review considerations that can hurt a score or delay an award if weak: human subjects protections, inclusion plans, vertebrate animals, biohazards, resubmission response, budget appropriateness, and resource and data sharing plans.

After review: percentile or impact score, summary statement, advisory council, then institute funding decisions against paylines and priorities. Only one resubmission (A1) is allowed, after which the work can return as a new application.

## 5. Timeline from idea to award

Standard receipt cycles for many R mechanisms are three per year (new R01s traditionally February 5, June 5, October 5; resubmissions a month later; K and other mechanisms on their own dates). Many NOFOs have their own dates. Expect roughly 8 to 10 months from submission to earliest start.

A workable backward plan for an R01:
- 12+ weeks out: aims page drafted and circulated; program officer contacted with the aims.
- 8 to 10 weeks: preliminary data figures fixed; biosketches started in SciENcv; subaward partners notified.
- 6 weeks: full Research Strategy draft; internal or external mock review.
- 4 weeks: budget final with the department administrator; letters requested.
- Internal routing deadline set by the UNM pre-award office (see `unm-data/institution/proposal-logistics.md`); the science is frozen after that in practice.
- Submit days early. eRA Commons errors and warnings take time to clear, and there is a two-day viewing window to correct errors only before the deadline.

## 6. Early-stage and new investigators

- An Early Stage Investigator is within 10 years of the terminal degree or end of clinical training and has not held a substantial independent NIH research award. ESI status applies to R01-equivalent applications and usually brings a more favorable payline. Confirm the status is correct in eRA Commons well before submission; extensions exist for leave and clinical training.
- Advise ESIs to use the status on their strongest R01 rather than spend years on small mechanisms that do not build toward one.
